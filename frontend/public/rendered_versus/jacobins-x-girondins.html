Jacobins x Girondins

French Revolution Factions
Corner A

Jacobins 1789–1794 (The Mountain)

The Jacobins (led by Maximilien Robespierre, Louis de Saint-Just, and Jean-Paul Marat). Radical Parisian revolutionaries occupying 'The Mountain'. Supported centralization of state power, price controls, the Committee of Public Safety, and the Reign of Terror to purge counter-revolutionaries.

VS
Corner B

Girondins 1791–1793 (The Plain)

The Girondins (led by Jacques Pierre Brissot and Madame Roland). Moderate provincial republicans from the Gironde region. Championed federalism, free-market economics, legal due process, opposed Parisian mob dominance, and voted against the immediate execution of King Louis XVI.

Key Differences & Divergences

  • Execution of Louis XVI: Demanded immediate death without appeal as a public enemy (Jacobins) VS Favored a national referendum or exile to preserve moderation (Girondins).
  • State Power: Extreme Parisian centralization and emergency dictatorial rule VS Decentralized provincial federalism and regional autonomy.
  • The Terror: The Jacobins orchestrated the June 1793 insurrection, arresting Girondin deputies and sending them en masse to the guillotine on October 31, 1793.
  • Downfall: Robespierre's unchecked Terror eventually consumed him at the Thermidorian Reaction in July 1794, sending him to the same blade.

Common Ground

Both began as allied members of the Jacobin Club, united in destroying the Ancien Régime monarchy to establish a French Republic.

The Verdict / Outcome

“The Jacobins violently crushed the Girondins in 1793, only to be destroyed by their own Reign of Terror a year later.”

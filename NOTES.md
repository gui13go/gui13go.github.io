Xuanzang — Chinese Buddhist monk who journeyed to India to retrieve Buddhist sutras (Journey to the West).
Brahmagupta — Indian mathematician who first formulated the mathematical rules for zero and negative numbers.
Tariq ibn Ziyad — Umayyad commander who led the 711 Islamic conquest of Hispania (Gibraltar namesake).

Charlemagne — King of the Franks crowned the first Holy Roman Emperor in 800, unifier of Western Europe.
Charles Martel — "The Hammer"; Frankish leader who halted Arab advance into Western Europe at the Battle of Tours.
Harun al-Rashid — Abbasid Caliph in Baghdad who established the House of Wisdom at the peak of Islamic civilization.
Li Bai — Tang Dynasty poetic genius celebrated for lyricism, spontaneity, and romantic imagery.
Du Fu — Revered Tang Dynasty poet who chronicled history, war, and the human condition.
Jabir ibn Hayyan (Geber) — Polymath and "Father of Chemistry" who pioneered distillation and experimental alchemy.

Alfred the Great — King of Wessex who defended England from the Vikings and revived law and learning.
Rurik — Varangian Viking chieftain who founded the Rurik Dynasty and laid the foundation of Kievan Rus'.
Al-Farabi — Islamic Golden Age philosopher and music theorist known in the East as the "Second Teacher".
Vladimir the Great — Grand Prince of Kiev who unified Kievan Rus' and adopted Orthodox Christianity in 988.
Olga of Kiev — Regent of Kievan Rus' famous for avenging her husband and introducing early Christianity to Rus'.

Pericles — Athenian statesman who led the Golden Age of Athens and oversaw the Parthenon.
Leonidas I — Spartan warrior-king who led the heroic last stand of the 300 at Thermopylae.
Herodotus — "Father of History" who chronicled the Greco-Persian Wars in The Histories.
Thucydides — "Father of scientific history" and political realism (Peloponnesian War).
Hippocrates — "Father of Medicine" who founded rational clinical medicine and the Hippocratic Oath.
Pythagoras — Pre-Socratic philosopher and mathematician (Pythagorean theorem, harmony of spheres).
Euclid — "Father of Geometry" whose Elements formed the axiomatic bedrock of mathematics.
Eratosthenes — Chief librarian of Alexandria who accurately measured the circumference of the Earth.
Democritus — "The Laughing Philosopher" who first formulated atomic theory and the void.

Caesar Augustus — First Emperor of Rome who established imperial rule and the 200-year Pax Romana.
Hannibal Barca — Carthaginian military mastermind who led elephants over the Alps and won at Cannae.
Scipio Africanus — Roman general who defeated Hannibal at Zama to secure Roman Mediterranean dominance.
Trajan — Roman Emperor who expanded the Roman Empire to its maximum territorial extent.
Hadrian — Emperor who fortified Roman borders (Hadrian's Wall) and rebuilt the Pantheon.
Nero — Infamous Roman Emperor remembered for extravagance, the Great Fire, and tyranny.
Virgil — Greatest Roman poet who composed the national epic The Aeneid.

Ramses II (the Great) — 19th-Dynasty Egyptian pharaoh, military strategist, and master builder (Abu Simbel).
Nebuchadnezzar II — King of Babylon who constructed the Hanging Gardens and the Ishtar Gate.
Darius the Great — Persian King of Kings who established the Royal Road and built Persepolis.
Sargon of Akkad — Ancient Mesopotamian conqueror who created history's first recorded empire.
Imhotep — Egyptian architect of the first pyramid (Step Pyramid of Djoser) and early healer.

Chanakya (Kautilya) — Maurya prime minister and author of the Arthashastra, the ancient treatise on realpolitik.
Sima Qian — Grand Historian of the Han Dynasty whose Shiji established Chinese historical writing.
Han Feizi — Foremost philosopher of Legalism, shaping the centralized imperial doctrine of China.

Johannes Gutenberg — Inventor of the movable type printing press in Europe, launching the print age.
Nicolaus Copernicus — Astronomer whose heliocentric model placed the Sun at the center of the universe.
Louis Braille — Educator who lost his sight as a boy and invented the raised-dot Braille alphabet.
Alexander Graham Bell — Scottish-American inventor awarded the first practical patent for the telephone.
Guglielmo Marconi — Nobel laureate who developed long-distance radio transmission and wireless telegraphy.

Andreas Vesalius — "Father of Modern Human Anatomy" who revolutionized medicine with De humani corporis fabrica.
William Harvey — Physician who first accurately discovered the systemic circulation of blood pumped by the heart.
Jean Piaget — Swiss psychologist who established the foundational stages of cognitive child development.
B.F. Skinner — Influential behaviorist psychologist who developed the principles of operant conditioning.

Thomas Jefferson — Principal author of the Declaration of Independence and third U.S. President.
Toussaint Louverture — Leader of the Haitian Revolution who established the first free Black republic.
José de San Martín — Argentine general and liberator of Argentina, Chile, and Peru from the Spanish Empire.
Toyotomi Hideyoshi — Peasant-born general who completed the unification of feudal Japan.

Antonio Vivaldi — Italian Baroque violin virtuoso and composer of The Four Seasons.
Pyotr Ilyich Tchaikovsky — Russian Romantic composer of Swan Lake, The Nutcracker, and Symphony No. 6.

Christopher Columbus — Genoese navigator whose 1492 transatlantic voyage initiated European colonization of the Americas.
James Cook — British explorer and cartographer who mapped the Pacific, New Zealand, and Australia.
Ernest Shackleton — Antarctic explorer celebrated for legendary leadership and survival in the Endurance expedition.

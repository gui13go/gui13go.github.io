Subsolar Point & Zenith

The subsolar point is the exact position on Earth's surface where the Sun is perceived directly overhead at the zenith (90° altitude angle). As Earth rotates, this point moves westward at 15 degrees of longitude per hour (roughly 1,670 km/h at the equator).

Equation of Time & Obliquity

Due to Earth's orbital eccentricity (0.0167) and the 23.44° axial tilt (obliquity), apparent solar noon varies from mean clock noon by up to ±16 minutes throughout the year. The solar calculations here model this analemma variation in real time.

Great Circle Night Geometry

Geometrically, the solar terminator is a great circle 90° away from the subsolar point. The dark hemisphere is rendered via spherical polygon coordinates centered on the antipodal point (λ + 180°, -δ), seamlessly projected onto any map coordinate system.
